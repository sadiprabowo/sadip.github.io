Notes on SWAN wave modelling setup

4 minute read · Sample post

At the heart of operational wave forecasting is a well-configured spectral wave model. SWAN (Simulating WAves Nearshore) is a third-generation wave model that I rely on for regional and nearshore applications — from the high swell environments of western Sumatra to coastal wave and run-up studies.

The essential inputs

Setting up a SWAN model requires a few key ingredients:

  • Computational domain — grid resolution and extent appropriate to the physics being resolved.
  • Bathymetry — clean, interpolated depths on the model grid.
  • Wind forcing — from atmospheric models or reanalysis.
  • Boundary conditions — from a coarser model or measurements.

Validation

No model is useful until it is validated against observations — wave buoys, altimetry, or other sources. I typically compare significant wave height, period, and direction, and compute standard skill metrics before trusting a setup for engineering use.
