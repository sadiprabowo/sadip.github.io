Under the original plan, the two Coastal Buoys (CB) of Lot 1 were to be deployed at Banda Neira and Bawean, while the eight Wave and Current Buoys (WCB) of Lot 2 were to be deployed at Merak, Bakauheni, Padangbai, Lembar, Kayangan, Pototano, Ketapang, and Gilimanuk. As the additional MMS-1 HPC Continuity Support Services are accommodated within the addendum, there were technical meetings between MMS-2 Package 3 Project Management and Secretary Unit with the Directorate for Marine Meteorology addressing the adjustment needs and the Maritime Meteorology Directorate providing proposed several alternatives of for the adjustment ing  of the MMS-2 Package 3 buoy implementation of MMS-2 Package 3, one of which is the relocation of the CB and WCB deployment sites (Minutes of Meeting No. e.B/ND/193/MMS-2.P3/X/2026, 30 September 2026 and …). The adjustment retains the full quantity of two Coastal Buoys and eight Wave and Current Buoys, and does not change the project goals or KPIs. 
The two Coastal Buoys were originally planned for deployment at Banda Neira and Bawean. Banda Neira is particularly challenging owing to its remoteness and lack of supporting infrastructure, which affects the deployment, maintenance, and recurring operational costs such as airtime and insurance. One CB is therefore redefined for deployment in the Seribu Islands Merak waters, a strategic location supporting the high-traffic vessel route and shipping lane around Jakarta waters and western part of the Java SeaMerak–Bakauheni ferry service, so that the observation needs there are met through the CB. The second CB remains at Bawean.
The technical considerations for this relocation are: 
Filling observation data gaps. Seribu Islands waters or western part of the Java Sea Tanjung Priok is an important location in the BMKG marine observation networkroadmap, yet it currently has no suitable instrument for continuous wave and current monitoring.
Supporting meteorological services for a major national port. Tanjung Priok is one of the main ports and a center of national logistics activity, and wave and current data from this area are expected to strengthen maritime meteorological information and port weather services, including the development of the Ina-PoWS service.
Availability of prior experience and baseline data. BMKG conducted ADCP observations around in the western part of the Java Sea or Seribu Islands watersTanjung Priok area during … period2018 to 2020, and the resulting data and experience can serve as an initial reference for the implementation and evaluation of the WCB.
Ease of operation and maintenance access. Seribu Islands watersTanjung Priok is relatively close to the BMKG Head Office, which facilitates monitoring, inspection, maintenance, and fault handling, and the Jakarta urban area has relatively good cellular network coverage for data transmission.
